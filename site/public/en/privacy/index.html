Privacy

This page explains what the public demo collects, where each item is processed and how long it is kept. It is a portfolio project and uses a fictitious company.

Applies to the current version of the demo, which runs on Cloudflare.

What the demo collects

  • The score from 0 to 10, the area and the tenure you pick.
  • The comment, if you write one. It is anonymized before it is stored and before it reaches the model.
  • The page language and the date and time of the submission.
  • The result of the bot check. To run that check, Cloudflare Turnstile processes your IP address, the TLS fingerprint of the connection and your browser's user agent. The token is verified and dropped. Details in the Turnstile privacy policy.

Where the data is processed

The demo runs entirely on Cloudflare. No part of it runs on a home server.

  • Worker: the code that receives the answer, anonymizes the comment and stores the result.
  • Workers AI: the Cloudflare service that runs the Gemma 4 26B A4B model and classifies the anonymized text.
  • D1: Cloudflare's database, which holds the anonymized text, the score, area, tenure and the classification.
  • Turnstile: the bot check.

Processing and storage happen outside Brazil. Cloudflare lists companies in the United States and England as sub-processors for Workers AI (sub-processor list), and D1 offers no location in Brazil or South America (D1 data location). This is an international data transfer, which is why the form says so before you submit.

Controller and processor

The person in charge of the project, Andrei Ludescher, is the controller (controlador under the LGPD): he decides what is collected and why. Cloudflare is the processor (operador): it processes the data on the controller's behalf. The Cloudflare data processing addendum puts it in these words: “the Customer is the Controller” and “Cloudflare is a Processor”. The addendum does not mention the LGPD. Cloudflare explains its view of the law on its LGPD page. Turnstile is an exception: Cloudflare also uses the bot check signals to improve its own bot detection, and for that use it states that it is a controller (Turnstile privacy policy).

What is anonymized and how

The comment is anonymized inside the Worker, before it is stored and before it reaches the model. Fixed rules find documents, contact details and addresses by their patterns and check digits. A list of names from IBGE, the Brazilian statistics office, finds personal names. The demo does not use a name recognition model, so place names slip through more easily than in local mode. Only the text with placeholders reaches Workers AI.

What Cloudflare does with the text

  • Cloudflare states that it does not use content processed by Workers AI to train models: “Cloudflare does not use Customer Content processed by Workers AI to train AI models” (Workers AI data usage).
  • For standard inference requests, like the ones in this demo, it states that it does not keep inputs or outputs after delivering the response: “does not retain inputs or outputs after they are no longer needed to process the request” (same page). The same page lists exceptions, such as a legal obligation.
  • It may keep technical metadata, such as timestamps, the model used and token counts, without the content.
  • The Worker never writes comment text to its logs.

What is never stored

  • The original comment text.
  • Your IP address. It is the key of Cloudflare's submission rate limiter, which keeps only short lived counters, and it is never written to the database. To count distinct people per day, the Worker stores a code (hash) of the IP mixed with a value that changes every day. These codes are deleted the next day.
  • Tracking cookies or audience analytics. The site uses none.

How long data is kept

  • Public submissions: deleted after 30 days by a daily job of the Worker.
  • Alert log: only an identifier, the case types and timestamps, deleted after 90 days.
  • Daily codes used to count distinct people: deleted the next day.
  • Rows deleted from D1 can still be restored for 7 days through Time Travel, a database feature that is always on (D1 Time Travel). In practice, a submission is gone for good within 37 days.
  • Technical records of the API calls (Workers Logs), without comment text: Cloudflare keeps them for 3 days (Workers Logs).
  • Daily counters without any text, such as number of submissions and analysis time: kept for the demo statistics.

Minimum group

No group, cross tab or slice with fewer than 5 answers is shown on the dashboard. Small areas are merged into one unnamed group. When that group is small too, the smallest visible area joins it, so nobody can find a hidden group by subtracting the others from the total.

Local mode

For organizations that cannot let anything leave their own infrastructure, the same system runs with docker compose on a company server: anonymization with rules, the IBGE list and spaCy name recognition, classification with Gemma 4 E2B on Ollama, an SQLite database and n8n automations. In this mode, no comment goes to Cloudflare or any other service, except alert channels the company itself turns on, such as Telegram.

Suggested legal basis for a real company

In this demo, the basis is your consent, given with the confirmation box on the form, which also says that the analysis happens outside Brazil. In a real company in Brazil, the most common basis for a climate survey is legitimate interest (LGPD, art. 7, IX), with a documented balancing test and the right not to answer. Comments may contain sensitive data, such as health or sexual orientation, which needs a basis from art. 11. When the survey supports the psychosocial risk management required by NR-1, compliance with a legal obligation may be considered. A company that uses Cloudflare, like this demo, also has to handle the international transfer (LGPD, art. 33). The local mode avoids that transfer.

This is a portfolio project and not legal advice. A real company should confirm the legal basis with its data protection officer and legal team.
